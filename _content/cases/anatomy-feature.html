Lessons in Anatomy

From written word to moving image.

I developed an original poem into an illustrated short for Cold Feet Editions. The film follows a boat, a shoal and an immortal jellyfish before returning to shore. I wanted the images to carry the poem’s intimacy into a format that could introduce readers to the studio.

  2. 0:08–0:28. A shoal drifts through the dark water. A rose-coloured jellyfish comes forward. The words “the first immortal creature” appear beneath it.
  3. 0:28–0:47. An anatomical heart overlaps the jellyfish. A rose wash gives way to fabric and bare feet in the surf.
  4. 0:47–0:51. Warm paper rises across the shoreline. The words “unfinished business” remain briefly over an illustrated waterline.
  5. 0:51–0:54. The end card reads “COLD FEET EDITIONS”, “Words almost swallowed.” and “coldfeeteditions.store”.

Explore the visual language and production
Drawing the invisible.

I used fine contours and denser shading to bring the jellyfish and heart into the same visual world. The shore carries the film’s rose tones back into the body. Warm paper and restrained italic type let the last fragment linger.

The annotated print shows how related motifs carry into the published artwork.

V2 already follows a descent and return. In V5 I condensed the same broad journey from 70 to 54 seconds. These matched moments show where I changed the visual treatment.
