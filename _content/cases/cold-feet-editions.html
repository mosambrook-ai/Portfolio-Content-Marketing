Chapter 01

The challenge

Give original poetry a life beyond the page through products people can use, display or give.

I needed to make an intimate literary brand approachable for readers and gift buyers. With a small budget and one person running the studio, the work relied on organic search, repeatable content and a clear path to purchase.

What I took on

  • Brand identity and creative direction
  • Website design and copywriting
  • Product development and presentation
  • SEO and Google Search Console
  • Editorial content and storytelling
  • Pinterest content marketing
  • TikTok content creation
  • Email marketing through Mailchimp
  • Audience development and marketing strategy

Chapter 02

Building the brand

Positioning

Intimate, but easy to buy.

I wanted poetry to feel personal and still be easy to buy, give or display.

Poems are private; products are publicThe poems are set on objects people can own and give: framed prints, a hoodie, a desk mat and a hardback.
No budget for paid reachEvery piece of content does more than one job: it builds the brand, can be found in search and points to a product.
A dark-romantic tone, a broad gift marketThe mood stays restrained: warm paper, one deep wine, clear product names and plain paths to buy.
A small studio asking for trustA Journal and a consistent identity show a person with a point of view, not just a catalogue.

Visual identity and product design

Two voices, one page.

Expressive serif type, handwritten annotation, botanical illustration, warm paper and restrained dark-romantic detail.

Annotated: one print, five decisions
  1. Two voices on one page. A handwritten opening line sits over typeset body text. The poet speaks first; the editor sets the rest.
  2. An illustration that answers the poem. The jellyfish is the poem’s subject, drawn once and small in the margin rather than used as decoration.
  3. The circled word. A teacher’s correction mark, repeated on the hoodie. One small device makes separate products read as one range.
  4. Ink-wash sea as base note. The only cool tone on a warm page, so the poem has somewhere to settle.
  5. A quiet maker’s mark. Wine rules, a compass star and the studio name close every print.
The products carry the identity

Website and user experience

A shop that reads like a magazine.

The site brings the brand first, then the objects, then the stories behind them. Visit coldfeeteditions.store.

{{STOREFRONT_EVIDENCE}}
How the site supports discovery and purchase
  1. Communicate the brand immediately. An editorial hero with wordmark, tagline and botanical art, so a visitor knows the world before scrolling.
  2. Help visitors understand the products. Named collections with a single line of copy each, and product pages that show the object and the poem together.
  3. Connect original writing to physical products. The poem’s own lines appear on the print, hoodie and desk mat, so the writing is the product, not a caption.
  4. Encourage product exploration. Navigation for All Products, Collections, Journal, About and Contact, with collections such as The Things We Carry.
  5. Build trust through editorial storytelling. The Journal and About pages show who is behind the studio and how the work is made.
  6. Support subscriptions and repeat visits. Newsletter sign-up beside the content people come back for: new essays, releases and seasonal collections.

Chapter 06

Measuring what matters

I set up Google Search Console. The case study currently includes qualitative learnings only. The SEO dataset describes estimated search demand.

What I learned

Limited budget made content do double duty: the same poem, image or essay had to build the brand, be findable and lead to a product.

One small device, the circled word, links a print and a hoodie. A recurring mark made separate products read as one range.

{{MEASUREMENT_STRATEGY}}

Next priorities

Explore proposed experiments
Proposed experiment
  • Same idea, three doors. Publish one poem line as a TikTok, a Pinterest pin and a Journal excerpt, each with a tagged link. Compare which leads most visitors to subscribe. Measure: Tagged-link clicks and sign-ups.
  • Lead magnet. Offer a free poem or desktop wallpaper at sign-up and compare the sign-up rate with the current form over the same length of time. Measure: Mailchimp sign-ups.
  • Gift guide refresh. Update the poetry gifts guide with a link to every product before the gifting season, then compare its clicks and impressions with the previous period. Measure: Search Console.
  • Subject-line test. Send two styles of subject line for Notes from the Margins, one poetic and one plain, to halves of the list. Measure: Mailchimp A/B result.
