Illustrated poetry film and print · Writing, creative direction, visual storytelling

Explore the storyboard and production process

Intended audience. The Literary Romantic and Cultural Reader.

Creative concept. The immortal jellyfish gives the poem a way into mortality and emotional vulnerability. I carried the marine imagery into the print through an ink-wash sea and a small jellyfish study. The circled word and handwritten opening link the visual treatment to the poem’s anatomy-lesson conceit.

My contribution. I wrote the poem and set the visual direction. The annotated print shows how I placed the handwriting, recurring marks and illustration around the text. I also set the direction for the illustrated film and reviewed iterations for pacing, continuity and tone. Production used AI-assisted development and a Codex and Remotion animation workflow.

Time ranges mark editorial beats. Dissolves overlap across those boundaries.

What changed from V2. The 70-second version already moves through the water and back to shore. V5 condenses that arc to 54 seconds, pairs a stormier sea with denser rose-toned anatomy and brings fabric and moving foam into the shoreline scene. The storyboard PDF includes three matched comparisons.

Distribution plan. Use excerpts on TikTok and a jellyfish quote pin on Pinterest to introduce the poem. A Journal piece could share the work’s inspiration and lead readers to the print.

Product marketing and creative advertising

The page you almost wrote.

Position the Almost, Always journal as a place to begin writing, even when the words are difficult to find.

Product design and short-form ad concept · Positioning, copywriting, creative direction

Explore the Almost, Always campaign

Intended audience. The Aspiring Writer and Literary Romantic.

Creative concept. Move from hesitation to possibility. Pomegranate artwork gives the journal a recognisable visual motif. Restrained movement and an open spread give the viewer time to imagine writing the first line.

My contribution. I developed the product positioning and a planned 20-second sequence. The opening pomegranate detail leads to a cover reveal, then the journal opens. A single page turn gives way to a pause on a blank spread and a final product reveal.

Intended next step. Invite viewers to discover the journal on its product page. The short-form sequence was developed as an advertising concept.

Explore the Revolution essay campaign

Intended audience. The Cultural Reader.

Creative concept. I used the legend of Julie d’Aubigny to consider queer love, self-censorship and the distance between ordinary desires and the extraordinary acts queer stories can demand. The illustration visualises the imagined escape that opens the essay.

My contribution. I wrote the long-form Journal essay and adapted it into a TikTok carousel. The original essay holds the full argument; the social cover gives readers a way into it.
