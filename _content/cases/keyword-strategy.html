The architecture of discovery.

Turning search behaviour into strategy

I conducted UK-focused keyword research using Semrush to understand how potential customers discover literary gifts, poetry products and creative accessories.

Rather than targeting keywords solely on search volume, I evaluated keyword difficulty, search intent and relevance to the existing product range. This revealed opportunities to prioritise lower-competition, niche searches while developing a longer-term strategy for broader literary gifting terms.

I translated the research into a structured keyword map, assigning primary and secondary keywords to product pages, collections and editorial content. The resulting strategy connects organic discovery with meaningful content and clear pathways to purchase.

Keyword explorer

Keyword difficulty (KD) is Semrush’s estimate of how hard it may be to rank in Google’s top ten. A higher percentage indicates more competition. Search intent describes what someone wants to do: learn, compare options, act or find a specific site. Some terms serve more than one intent.

{{ROADMAP}}
Research methodology
  1. Conduct UK-focused keyword discovery in Semrush.
  2. Identify relevant product and audience search terms.
  3. Evaluate search volume, keyword difficulty and intent.
  4. Remove irrelevant and competitor-specific terms.
  5. Organise keywords into thematic clusters.
  6. Assign primary and secondary terms to relevant pages.
  7. Prioritise by relevance, competition and strategic opportunity.
